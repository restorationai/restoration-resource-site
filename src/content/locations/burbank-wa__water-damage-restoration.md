A lot of Burbank's housing stock sits on slab or crawlspace foundations built for a drier, more rural setting, not for sustained water exposure. Properties near the river and the irrigation canals that run through this part of the county deal with a naturally higher water table during spring runoff, which means crawlspaces can stay damp longer after a plumbing failure than they would on higher, better-drained ground. Add in the number of manufactured and site-built homes on private well systems, and a pressure tank or supply line failure can go unnoticed for hours because there's no municipal shutoff to flag the problem. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Burbank

The first call is always about stopping the source and getting water out fast. Crews extract standing water, pull wet carpet pad or compromised flooring material, and check for water that's traveled under baseboards or into wall cavities, which happens often in homes with older plumbing runs. Because many Burbank properties are on well and septic, we also check whether the loss has affected the septic system or leach field before we start drying, since pushing more water into a saturated field can make things worse. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: we set air movers and dehumidifiers based on moisture readings, not guesswork, and we re-check daily until materials are back to a dry standard, not just dry to the touch.
