## Why Kahlotus Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Kahlotus sits in the Channeled Scablands of the lower Columbia Basin, a landscape of exposed basalt and thin topsoil shaped by ancient floodwater. There's little natural windbreak out here, so straight-line wind events and sudden squalls moving off the Snake River corridor hit buildings at full force. A lot of the housing stock is older farmhouses and outbuildings built for wheat and dryland agriculture, with metal roofing, wood-frame additions, and detached shops or grain storage that weren't engineered for modern wind-load standards. When a storm tears a seam in a metal roof or knocks a tree limb through a porch overhang, water follows the path of least resistance into attic insulation and wall cavities fast, often before anyone's home to notice. Hail and wind-driven rain also find their way behind older wood siding and under loose flashing on barns and sheds that haven't been re-roofed in decades.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Kahlotus

The first call is about stopping the damage from spreading. That usually means emergency tarping over a torn roof section or a broken window, and board-up on any opening a storm has left exposed to weather or wildlife. From there we move to debris and downed-limb clearing around the structure so our crew and your insurance adjuster can safely get a full look at the damage. Moisture mapping comes next, we use the same documentation standard (IICRC S500) whether we're drying a farmhouse kitchen or a detached shop, checking subfloor, wall cavities, and insulation for trapped water that isn't visible from the surface. Structural drying follows with commercial air movers and dehumidification sized to the space, and we don't call a job closed until moisture readings in the affected materials are back to a dry standard, not just "looks dry."
