## What do you do in the first 60 seconds?

Shut off the main water valve immediately, then cut power to anything the water is touching before you touch it.

Most Pasco homes have the main shutoff near the water meter at the street side of the property, or where the supply line enters the garage. If you can't find it fast, shut the valve under the nearest fixture, a toilet, sink, or washing machine, to slow the flow while you locate the main.

If water is pooling near outlets, a breaker panel, or light fixtures, go to the panel and cut power to that circuit. Do not flip a wall switch while standing in water. Once the water's stopped and the power's safe, move what you can off the wet floor (rugs, boxes, electronics) and stop touching anything else until help arrives.

## Who do you call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call both, close together. A licensed plumber stops the water at its source; a restoration company removes the standing water and starts drying before mold and structural damage set in.

A plumber's job is the pipe itself: find the break, repair or replace the section, confirm the line holds pressure again. That's it. They aren't equipped to extract hundreds of gallons from a subfloor or run commercial dehumidifiers for three days. You can verify any plumber's Washington license through the [Washington L&I contractor verification tool](https://secure.lni.wa.gov/verify/) before they start work.

A restoration company's job starts the moment water hits your drywall, flooring, and insulation. Left wet, those materials support mold growth within 24 to 48 hours of the water intrusion, per the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). Drying needs to start the same day, not whenever a contractor has an opening next week.

## What happens in the first 24 hours?

The first 24 hours are extraction, assessment, and setup, not full repairs; full drying usually takes several more days after that.

1. **Extraction.** Standing water gets pulled out with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, usually within the first hour or two of arrival.
2. **Moisture mapping.** A technician uses a moisture meter to map exactly how far the water traveled, including inside walls and under flooring, so nothing wet gets missed.
3. **Containment and demo where needed.** Baseboards, wet drywall, or saturated insulation that can't dry in place come out, following the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) for water damage restoration.
4. **Drying equipment placed.** Air movers and low-grain-refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers go in to pull moisture out of structural materials, monitored daily with meter readings until everything hits a dry standard.
5. **Documentation.** Photos, moisture logs, and a written scope get compiled, this is what your insurance adjuster will want to see.

A burst pipe that's caught fast, water shut off within minutes, usually starts out as Category 1 (clean) water. That water doesn't stay clean once it sits on building materials or soaks into flooring for hours, so faster shutoff and faster extraction both matter for keeping the cleanup simpler.

## Will homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in Pasco?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, including a pipe that bursts without warning, but check your specific policy for exclusions.

What's typically NOT covered: damage from a leak that's been dripping for months, or damage tied to poor maintenance. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) outlines this distinction in more detail. You have a duty to mitigate once the water stops, meaning starting drying promptly protects your claim, and documenting everything with photos and receipts before and during cleanup matters. Our [plain-English guide to homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) covers deductibles and claim timing if you want to read ahead before the adjuster calls.

If you're not sure whether this is covered, start the mitigation anyway. Insurers expect you to stop further damage regardless of how the claim shakes out, and a documented scope of work from a restoration company backs up what actually happened.
