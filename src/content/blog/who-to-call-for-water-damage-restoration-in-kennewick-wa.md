## Who do I call first for water damage in Kennewick?

Call a licensed, IICRC-certified restoration company before you call your insurance company. A restoration crew stops the spread of damage immediately: pulling standing water, setting containment, and documenting the loss for your claim.

Your insurer will ask what you did to mitigate the damage. "I called a restoration company the same day" is a much stronger answer than "I waited for the adjuster." Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or a failed appliance hose, but the clock on mold growth starts within 24 to 48 hours on wet drywall and carpet padding per the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), so the restoration call comes first.

## When is it a plumber or utility call instead?

Call whichever number you can reach fastest if water is still actively running, because stopping the source matters more than who stops it. If you can already shut off the water at the fixture or the main, do that first, then call.

A few situations need a different first call entirely. If you smell gas, see sparks, or water is near an electrical panel, call 911 or your utility before anyone enters the space. If the leak is from a city main or a street-side break, that's a call to the water utility, not a restoration company. For everything else, in Kennewick, [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) and [water cleanup](/services/water-cleanup/) are the services that handle extraction, drying, and the structural side once the source is controlled.

## When should I loop in my insurance company?

Call your insurer once mitigation has started, not before. Most carriers want the loss stopped and documented first; calling mid-flood rarely gets you anywhere useful and can waste the window when damage is still containable.

Keep receipts, take your own photos before anything is moved, and ask the restoration crew for a written scope of work. Standard homeowners policies generally cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe or failed appliance line, but exclude gradual leaks and sewer backups unless you carry a separate water-backup endorsement; the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) covers this in plain language if you want to check your policy language before the adjuster calls.
